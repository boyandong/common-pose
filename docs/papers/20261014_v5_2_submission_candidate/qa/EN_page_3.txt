E. Cohorts and Statistical Analysis
The two previously examined evaluation cohorts comprise
A20 (20 participants; 1,650,789 valid measured–estimated
pairs) and N80 (80 participants; 7,111,408 pairs). They are
analyzed separately and are not treated as independent con-
ﬁrmation after repeated examination. Participants used for
selection-scorer development are separate from the reported
evaluation participants; this separation does not certify the
upstream estimator’s training membership.
The exact training records associated with the released
upstream checkpoint could not be established. Public training
metadata contain train records for 70 of 80 N80 participants
and 16 of 20 A20 participants, but metadata overlap does not
by itself prove exposure of the actual checkpoint. We therefore
make no veriﬁed upstream unseen-participant generalization
claim.
Participants, not blocks, are the statistical units. Method
differences are calculated within each participant before tak-
ing the median. Descriptive 95% intervals for paired me-
dian effects use 10,000 participant-level bootstrap resamples.
Consumers and retention budgets share participants and are
dependent comparisons; the intervals are pointwise descriptive
summaries rather than family-wise simultaneous conﬁdence
statements. Balanced comparisons use only jointly evaluable
participants and report their counts.
III. RESULTS
A. Decision Disagreement Under Pose Substitution
Pose substitution changed decisions for every frozen con-
sumer. Without rejection, N80 participant-median disagree-
ment ranged from 16.43% for SHOW instruction to 43.05%
for CeTI posture (Table I). The variation shows that different
consumers can respond differently to the same paired target
pose inputs.
Reference-class support was already uneven before selec-
tion. All ﬁxed classes met the predeﬁned minimum in 22/80
participants for DB9 posture and 79/80 for KIN phase, versus
80/80 for the other four consumers. These differences matter
when interpreting class-balanced outcomes after rejection.
B. Selective Ranking Across Frozen Consumers
Full/U ranking reduced overall disagreement across all six
consumers in N80 at 30% ofﬂine retention. Relative to Bound-
ary, the median within-participant reduction ranged from 1.867
percentage points for DB9 grasp (95% descriptive bootstrap
interval: 1.431–2.371) to 4.651 points for DB9 posture (2.784–
6.505). All six pointwise intervals were above zero (Table I).
Across the four tested retention budgets, Full/U showed
positive median paired improvements over Boundary in all
24 consumer–budget comparisons within each of A20 and
N80 (Fig. 2). These comparisons share participants and related
experimental conditions, so the direction counts are descriptive
rather than independent conﬁrmations.
Direct paired comparisons favored Full/U over Score-only/U
in 23/24 N80 and 22/24 A20 consumer–budget settings, with
non-improving conditions retained in the analysis. For CeTI
grasp at 30% retention in N80, the direct within-participant
Full/U improvement over Score-only/U was 3.790 percent-
age points (95% descriptive bootstrap interval: 2.774–4.691;
n=80).
