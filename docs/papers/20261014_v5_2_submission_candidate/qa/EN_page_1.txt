Decision Fidelity Across Frozen Pose Consumers
Under EMG-Based Input Substitution
Boyan Dong
School of Biological Science and Medical Engineering
Southeast University
Nanjing, China
yan494815@gmail.com
Abstract—Hand pose may provide a shared interface between
electromyography (EMG) sensing and models trained on pose
data. Y et an input that ﬁts a model does not necessarily
preserve its decisions. We investigate this gap using one frozen
EMG-to-pose estimator and six frozen pose classiﬁers built
from four public datasets. By comparing decisions on paired
measured and estimated poses, we ﬁrst quantify how often
input substitution changes the outputs of unchanged classiﬁers.
We then test whether learned ofﬂine ranking can preferentially
retain decisions that match the measured-pose reference. Across
80 participants, full-feature ranking reduced median paired
overall disagreement by 1.87–4.65 percentage points relative to
boundary-based selection at 30% retention. However, this overall
improvement did not always extend to individual reference
classes. For SHOW instruction, overall disagreement improved
by 2.61 points while class-balanced disagreement worsened by
20.88 points among the same 79 evaluable participants. Selec-
tion also reduced evaluable class support in some consumers.
These ﬁndings show that lower overall disagreement does not
guarantee class-balanced decision preservation, motivating joint
evaluation of overall disagreement, class-balanced disagreement,
and reference-class support. The results characterize retrospec-
tive agreement with model-generated reference decisions, not
independent action accuracy or validated online rejection.
I. INTRODUCTION
Electromyography (EMG) is commonly used to decode
movement or gestures directly. An alternative is to estimate
hand pose from EMG and pass that estimate to models already
trained on pose data. But the input may be compatible in
format without preserving the downstream model’s decision.
A ﬁxed gesture classiﬁer may predict class A from mea-
sured hand pose but class B from the corresponding EMG-
estimated pose. We call this change a decision ﬂip: input
substitution changed the model’s output, regardless of whether
either prediction matches an independently labeled action. This
motivates evaluating downstream decisions as well as pose
reconstruction error.
Previous work has studied EMG-based pose estimation [ 1],
pose-guided EMG representations [ 2], [ 3], and rejection in
myoelectric or selective prediction systems [ 4]–[6]. Here, we
keep the upstream estimator and downstream classiﬁers ﬁxed.
We examine three connected questions: How often does
pose substitution change frozen downstream decisions? Can
ofﬂine ranking reduce these changes? And do improvements
in overall agreement extend across reference classes? Our
evaluation concerns output ﬁdelity rather than independently
veriﬁed action accuracy or online control.
II. METHODS
A. Frozen Pose-Input Substitution and Consumers
A frozen pose consumer is a pose-based classiﬁer whose
parameters and source-ﬁtted preprocessing remain unchanged.
We pass measured pose and EMG-estimated pose through
the same consumer and compare the resulting decisions.
The upstream EMG-to-pose estimator is also frozen. Our six
linear discriminant analysis (LDA) consumers cover posture
and grasp classiﬁcation from DB9 [ 7] and CeTI-Age [ 8],
instruction classiﬁcation from SHOW3D [ 9], and movement-
phase classiﬁcation from KIN-MUS UJI [ 10].
The released VEMG2Pose estimator [ 1] and each source
consumer are connected using a ﬁxed eight-joint pose adapter
in radians. Joint selection, units, and available axis or topol-
ogy information were audited. The paired target inputs use
corresponding cached block indices after a common crop,
with approximately 20-ms target blocks. These implementa-
tion checks do not independently establish identical physical
joint zero positions or residual sensor synchronization across
sources.
The original EMG-to-pose dataset documents individual
calibration and software timestamp alignment [ 1]. Cross-
source physical equivalence remains a separate, unveriﬁed
requirement.
Six source-domain leave-one-subject-out evaluations were
reproduced against the archived out-of-fold predictions. The
corresponding balanced accuracies were 74.79%, 85.50%,
58.56%, 72.25%, 100%, and 56.17% for DB9 posture, DB9
grasp, CeTI posture, CeTI grasp, SHOW instruction, and KIN
phase, respectively. The SHOW result covers 16 participants
and 60 weak-instruction recordings. These tasks use different
source labels and evaluation units, so their accuracies should
not be interpreted as a common performance ranking.
